## 3.1 Tier 1: The "Function" Foundation (Cloze Exemption)
**Order of Implementation:** 1. Source a frequency dictionary for your target language.
2. Isolate the top 100 words (these will almost entirely be pronouns, prepositions, conjunctions, and existential verbs like *to be* or *to have*).
3. Do not use images. Build "Cloze Deletion" (fill-in-the-blank) flashcards that test these words within the "Golden 13" structural skeletons you built in Phase 2.

**Suggested Sources:** Routledge Frequency Dictionaries, Wiktionary Top 1000 lists, Clozemaster.

### Template: The Tier 1 Cloze Card Format
| Card Side | Field Content | Logic / Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Front** | Context Sentence: *Je ne mange [...] de viande.* (I do not eat [any] meat.) | Tests the functional grammar piece within a complete thought. |
| **Front** | Target Hint (Optional): *[any]* | Provides the structural target without translating the whole sentence. |
| **Back** | Answer: ***pas*** | The exact piece of missing glue. |
| **Back** | L1 Safety Net: *pas de = any / none* | Maps the grammatical translation solely for verification. |

## 3.2 Tier 2: The Action Engine (Verbs & Collocations)
**Order of Implementation:** 1. Identify the 100 most frequent verbs in the language.
2. Apply the "Companion Rule": Never write the verb down in isolation.
3. For each verb, find 2 or 3 common collocations (words that naturally pair with it) and learn the entire phrase as a single block.

**Suggested Sources:** Reverso Context (excellent for finding natural collocations), Linguee.

### Template: The Chunking Matrix
| Target Verb | Avoid (Isolated) | Good (The Functional Chunk) | Why this works (The Context) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **To Take (Prendre - FR)** | *Prendre* | *Prendre une décision* (To make a decision) | In French, you "take" a decision, you don't "make" it. Learning the chunk prevents L1 translation errors. |
| **To Do (Hacer - ES)** | *Hacer* | *Hace frío* (It is cold) | In Spanish, the weather "does" cold. The chunk bypasses the need to know why. |
| **To Hit (Dǎ - CN)** | *Dǎ* | *Dǎ dī* (To take a taxi) | "Dǎ" is a light verb with dozens of meanings. Chunks are the only way to lock down its specific usage. |

## 3.3 Tier 3: The "Concrete Core" (Contextual Clusters)
**Order of Implementation:** 1. Choose a "Contextual Story" (e.g., The Kitchen Routine).
2. Isolate 5-10 highly visual, concrete nouns related to that specific event.
3. Create pure visual Anki cards. No English on the front, no English on the back.

**Suggested Sources:** Google Images (search using the target language word to get culturally accurate images), Anki image occlusion features.

### Template: The Contextual Story Map

| :--- | :--- | :--- |

## 3.4 Tier 4: The Personal Identity Layer (The "Niche" 75)
**Order of Implementation:** 1. Write out a literal "Day in the Life" paragraph in your native language, detailing your actual job and hobbies.
2. Highlight the 75 most crucial niche nouns/verbs you need to talk about yourself.
3. Translate these highly specific terms, ideally verifying them with a native speaker to ensure you have the industry-standard jargon.

**Suggested Sources:** DeepL Translator, ChatGPT (Prompt: "I am a [Job]. What are the most natural ways to translate these industry terms into [Language]?"), Native language exchange partners.

### Template: The Niche Audit
| Domain | L1 Reality | Target Translation | Context/Usage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Career (Tech)** | "I need to fix a bug in the code." | *Debo corregir un error en el código.* (Spanish) | Avoids sounding like a textbook; lets you speak naturally with colleagues. |
| **Hobby (Music)** | "I play the acoustic guitar." | *Je joue de la guitare acoustique.* (French) | Ensures you know the specific preposition (*de la*) needed for your exact hobby. |
| **Dietary/Health** | "I am allergic to peanuts." | *Wǒ duì huāshēng guòmǐn.* (Mandarin) | A critical, non-negotiable phrase customized to the learner's physical reality. |

## 3.5 The "Lego" Filler Table (Classifiers/Measures)
**Order of Implementation:** 1. Audit if your target language requires measure words between numbers and nouns.
2. Group them by their physical/visual characteristics (Flat, Long, Volume, People).
3. Append these to the Tier 3 Concrete Nouns so they are learned together.

**Suggested Sources:** Language-specific grammar guides (e.g., AllSet Learning for Mandarin Chinese Grammar).

### Template: The Classifier Blueprint
| Target Category | Classifier/Measure Word | Used For (Visual Shape/Concept) | Functional Chunk Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Flat Objects** | *Zhāng* (Mandarin) | Paper, tables, tickets, maps. | *Yī zhāng piào* (One ticket) |
| **Long/Thin Objects** | *Tiáo* (Mandarin) | Rivers, roads, snakes, pants. | *Yī tiáo lù* (One road) |
| **Bound Volumes** | *Satsu* (Japanese) | Books, magazines, dictionaries. | *Hon wo is-satsu* (One book) |

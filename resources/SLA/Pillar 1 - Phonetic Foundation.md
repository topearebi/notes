## 1.1 The Phonemic Audit (Inventory & Mechanics)
**Order of Implementation:** 1. Pull the complete IPA (International Phonetic Alphabet) chart for your target language.
2. Cross-reference it with your native language's IPA chart. 
3. Delete the matching sounds. 
4. For the remaining "New" sounds, research the specific physical tongue/lip placement.

**Suggested Sources:** Wikipedia's "Phonology of [Language]" pages, interactive IPA vowel charts, and YouTube videos from speech-language pathologists outlining target language pronunciation.

### Template: The "Mouth Hack" Matrix
| IPA Symbol | Script Rep | Description (Place/Manner) | L1 Status | The Physical "Mouth Hack" (Execution) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **/y/** | *u* (French) | Close front rounded vowel | **NEW** | Shape lips like a tight "O" (as if whistling), but push the tongue forward and try to say the English "EE". |
| **/ʁ/** | *r* (French) | Voiced uvular fricative | **NEW** | Rest the tip of the tongue behind the bottom teeth. Lift the back of the tongue toward the uvula and gently "gargle" air without vibrating vocal cords. |
| **/p/** | *p* (French) | Voiceless bilabial plosive | Known (but altered) | **Unaspirated:** Press lips together. Release the sound *without* the puff of air used in the English "p". Hold a piece of paper in front of your mouth; it should not move. |

## 1.2 Minimal Pair & Contrastive Diagnostic
**Order of Implementation:** 1. Identify the L1/L2 "merger conflicts" (sounds your brain thinks are the same, but the target language treats as different).
2. Gather audio of minimal pairs from native speakers.
3. Build your Anki ABX testing deck. 

**Suggested Sources:** Forvo.com (for downloading native audio snippets), Rhinospike, or pre-made Anki "Minimal Pair" shared decks.

### Template: The Conflict Log
| L1 Conflict Zone | Word A (Audio/IPA) | Word B (Audio/IPA) | The Physical Delta (The one thing that changes) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Vowel Rounding** | *rue* (/ʁy/ - street) | *roue* (/ʁu/ - wheel) | **Lip Position:** /y/ requires pushed-forward, tight lips. /u/ is relaxed. |
| **Aspiration** | *pill* (/pʰɪl/ - Eng) | *pile* (/pil/ - French) | **Airflow:** English releases a sharp puff of air (aspiration). French does not. |
| **Tone (Mandarin)** | *mā* (妈 - mother) | *mǎ* (马 - horse) | **Pitch Contour:** Word A is high and flat. Word B dips low and rises. |

## 1.3 Coarticulation & Sandhi (The "Contact" Rules)
**Order of Implementation:** 1. Research the morphophonological rules of the target language (how words change when connected).
2. Map the specific triggers (e.g., does a word ending in a consonant change when the next word starts with a vowel?).
3. Log these as "connected speech" flashcards, prioritizing audio over text.

**Suggested Sources:** Language-specific grammar wikis, native podcasts designed for learners (e.g., InnerFrench) where connected speech is clear but natural.

### Template: The "Contact" Rulebook
| Rule Type | Trigger Condition | Visual Example (Text) | Acoustic Reality (IPA/Execution) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Liaison (French)** | Silent final consonant + Next word starts with a vowel. | *les amis* (the friends) | **/lezami/** (The silent 's' wakes up and sounds like a 'z', attaching to the next word). |
| **Elision (French)** | Word ending in 'e' + Next word starts with a vowel. | *le arbre* -> *l'arbre* | **/laʁbʁ/** (The first vowel is dropped entirely to prevent a glottal stop). |
| **Sandhi (Mandarin)** | Two 3rd tones in a row. | *nǐ hǎo* (hello) | **ní hǎo** (The first word changes to a 2nd tone [rising] to prevent vocal strain). |

## 1.4 Prosody, Suprasegmentals & Isochrony
**Order of Implementation:** 1. Determine the "Clock" (isochrony). 
2. Determine the intonation rules for questions vs. statements.
3. Shadow native audio without words (just humming) to internalize the map.

**Suggested Sources:** Praat (free phonetic software to visually see pitch contours), shadowing YouTube videos at 0.75x speed. 

### Template: The Rhythm & Pitch Map
| Feature | Target Language Mechanics | Execution Note (How to practice it) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Clock (Isochrony)** | **Syllable-timed (French):** Every syllable gets the exact same duration. | Stop compressing unstressed vowels (the English "schwa"). Tap your finger on a desk with a completely even, metronomic beat while speaking. |
| **Intonation (Melody)** | **Final-Syllable Stress:** The pitch and slight lengthening only happen at the very end of a phrase. | Speak in a flat, monotone voice until the very last syllable of the sentence, then slightly raise the pitch. |
| **Question Logic** | **Pitch Rise:** Pitch raises sharply at the end of the sentence to indicate a yes/no question. | Exaggerate the upward glide at the end of the phrase when shadowing. |

## 1.5 The Training Log (SOP)
**Order of Implementation:** This is your daily habit tracker for the first 2-4 weeks. Execute this strictly before moving into heavy vocabulary or syntax. 

**Suggested Sources:** Anki (for ABX), Audacity (for Delayed Auditory Feedback), smartphone mirror/camera.

### Template: Daily Phonetic SOP (20 Minutes Total)
| Phase | Duration | Activity | Target Metric |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Perception** | 10 Mins | **Anki ABX Minimal Pairs:** Listen to Audio 1 and Audio 2, then identify Audio 3. | 90% accurate blind discrimination. |
| **Mechanics** | 5 Mins | **Mirror Work:** Look in a mirror and physically execute the "Mouth Hacks" for the 3 hardest L2 phonemes. | Conscious Approximation (slow, deliberate accuracy). |
| **Integration** | 5 Mins | **Prosodic Shadowing:** Put on a 2-minute native audio clip. "Hum" or mumble along, matching the pitch and the "Clock". | Complete synchronization with the native speaker's rhythm. |

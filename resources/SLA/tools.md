## 1. The Core Infrastructure (Cross-Pillar Tools)

These are the non-negotiable foundations that manage the flow of data across the entire system.

- **Anki (Desktop & Mobile):** The absolute core of your operation. It handles the Spaced Repetition System (SRS) algorithm. You will use it for Pillar 1 (ABX audio testing), Pillar 3 (Visual/Cloze cards), and Pillar 4 (Logging new vocabulary).
- **Obsidian or Notion:** Your central database. Use this to map your "Syntax Highway," log the "Golden 13," and track your "Personal Identity" vocabulary before porting it to Anki. Obsidian is highly recommended for its bidirectional linking (easily connecting a Tier 2 verb chunk directly to a Golden 13 syntax rule).

## 5. The AI Micro-Feedback Suite (Prompt Templates)

To execute the 5% spot-check without triggering the Affective Filter, use these exact prompts in ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.

### Prompt A: The Syntax Stress-Test (Pillar 2)
> "Act as a strict structural linguist. I am testing the default word order and syntax rules for [Target Language]. Review the following sentence: '[Insert Sentence]'. Do not rewrite the sentence to make it sound more poetic or native. Only correct it if I have broken a fundamental syntactical rule (e.g., placing the verb in the wrong position, or using the wrong negation marker). Explain the structural error in one sentence."

### Prompt B: The Collocation Check (Pillar 3)
> "Act as a native speaker of [Target Language]. I am trying to express the concept of '[Insert English Chunk/Concept, e.g., making a decision]'. I have translated this as '[Insert Target Language Chunk]'. Is this the standard, highest-frequency verb collocation used in everyday speech? If not, what is the exact 2-3 word chunk a native speaker would actually use?"

### Prompt C: The Substitution Auditor (Pillar 4)
> "I am doing a substitution drill in [Target Language]. The native skeleton sentence is: '[Insert Native Sentence]'. I have swapped out the nouns and verbs to fit my personal vocabulary. My new sentence is: '[Insert Your Sentence]'. Did my vocabulary swap break any hidden grammar rules, such as gender agreement, case endings, or required prepositions? Limit your feedback to a simple 'Yes, it works' or a specific identification of the broken rule."

Step 1: The Context Anchor
 * Identify the Domain: This text is situated in Post-Human Philosophy and Hard Science Fiction. It specifically deals with the "Identity Problem" in the context of mind uploading (Copy Theory) and digital immortality.
 * Identify the Core Tension: The central conflict is the Paradox of Continuity: the tension between radical self-optimization (transhumanism) and the preservation of the "self." It questions whether a version of a person that has been "healed" of their fundamental flaws is still the same person or a new entity entirely.

 * If he'd rebuilt himself, reinvented himself . . . then how much of the man she'd known remained? Had he granted himself transhuman resilience, and healed himself of his terminal despair...
Step 3: The Idea Incubator
 * Title: The Ship of Theseus in Code: The Myth of Digital Invariants
   * The Hook: If we upload our minds and edit out our "terminal despair," do we achieve salvation or merely commit a high-tech form of suicide to make room for a happier stranger?
   * The Evidence: "Where was the line? Between self-transformation... and death itself?"
 * Title: The Software Child: Inheritance vs. Identity
   * The Hook: A story about a digital construct that possesses its "father's" memories but views them as a historical database rather than lived experience, leading to a crisis of relationship with those the father loved.
   * The Evidence: "...a software child who'd merely inherited its father's memories."
 * Title: The Aesthetic Anchor: Why Taste Survives the Transition
   * The Hook: An essay exploring why Egan suggests that "aesthetic sensibilities" are more fundamental to the "core" of human identity than even our deepest psychological traumas.
   * The Evidence: "certain values, certain emotional responses, certain aesthetic sensibilities had survived..."

 * Metaphysics/Identity: In philosophy, the Psychological Continuity Identity Theory (Locke) suggests that memory is what makes us "us."

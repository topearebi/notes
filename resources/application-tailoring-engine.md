# Application Tailoring Engine — reusable procedure

**Use when:** tailoring a CV, cover letter, or job application; or when the
principal reports application fatigue.

Source artifacts live in `/workspace/documents/job-search/`:
`application-engine.md`, `master-experience-bank.md`, `grade-evidence-bank.md`.

---

## Core rule

**Tailoring is selection, not writing.** Maintain one master bank of pre-written,
evidence-tagged blocks. A new application = pick 5 blocks + rewrite a 2-line
header. Never start from a blank page.

## Why (behavioural diagnosis)

The friction is the *decision cost* of the blank page, not the prose. ~40 fresh
micro-decisions per bespoke CV is what burns people out. Remove the blank page
and the task collapses.

Cue → routine → reward: posting appears → run the 6-step protocol → submitted in
one sitting. Never rely on motivation; rely on the bank being pre-loaded.

## Procedure

1. Paste the JD into the bank's JD scratchpad. (2 min)
2. Highlight the 6–8 verbs/nouns the employer repeats — repetition is their real
   priority list. (2 min)
3. Match those words against the bank's `TAGS:` lines. (3 min)
4. Select the top 5 blocks, ordered by the role's top two priorities — not
   chronologically. (3 min)
5. Swap only the 2-line header to mirror their language. Do not edit blocks.
   (2 min)
6. Export. (3 min)

## Selection rules

- 5 blocks maximum. A CV is a shortlist, not an archive.
- Every block carries a number. No metric = cut it.
- Never write a new block for a single application. A missing-evidence posting is
  a *bank gap* — log it, fill it that week, move on.
- Two different framings of one achievement = two blocks, both written once.

## Recurring cost

20 minutes a week adding new achievements while fresh. That is the entire
maintenance burden. Skipping a week is fine; do not "catch up".

## UK Civil Service shortcut — zero tailoring

Internal promotion boards do not use a CV. They assess against published Success
Profiles (Behaviours, Experience, Technical, Strengths, Ability). One set of
examples, written once, is reused across every board and department indefinitely.
It is the only UK application system where tailoring is genuinely optional.

For SEO/G7: build 5 deep examples, not nine. The same example re-framed scores
against multiple behaviours. SEO-level examples must show *judgement and
influence*, not activity — an HEO example says "I delivered X on time", an SEO
example says "the programme was failing, I re-scoped it, persuaded X, and it
landed Y".

## Pitfalls

- Editing blocks per application defeats the whole system — it re-creates the
  blank page. If a block needs editing, it was written badly; fix it once.
- Pure academia is weak evidence. Frame degrees only where they produced an
  applied workplace change.
- Public-sector candidates usually have no process-improvement block. If the
  principal has one, it is his strongest private-sector transferable.
- Grade ceilings are hard: no pay rise gets you past a grade band. Diagnose
  grade first, then salary.

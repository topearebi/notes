## Sourcing High-Quality Music with slskd
### Introduction
Welcome to your comprehensive guide for using `slskd` to build a high-quality digital music library. The Soulseek network is a vast and incredible resource, but its uncurated nature can be daunting for newcomers. This guide provides a structured methodology, taking you from initial setup to advanced, automated sourcing.
The philosophy here is simple: use `slskd` as a powerful tool for one job and one job only—sourcing the best possible "raw ingredients." All the final organisation, tagging, and polishing will be handled by your powerful library manager, `beets.io`. By following this guide, you will learn how to find exactly what you want, verify its quality, and do so efficiently and respectfully within the Soulseek community.
## Part 1: The Foundation - Strategy & Standards
Before you download a single file, you need a plan. A clear strategy is the difference between building a pristine, manageable library and ending up with a chaotic folder of random files. This section establishes the core philosophy and technical standards that will govern every file you acquire.
### 1.1 The Library Philosophy: A Two-Prong Approach
**The Concept:** Digital storage is finite. While it's tempting to want the absolute best quality for every track, the storage cost of a fully lossless library can be immense. We will therefore adopt a pragmatic and popular strategy that balances perfect archival quality with everyday usability.
- **Archival Tier (FLAC):** This tier is for the albums that matter most to you. Think of these as your "digital master copies" or "first editions." They are stored in a lossless format (FLAC), which is a perfect, bit-for-bit copy of the original source (like a CD). This guarantees zero quality loss and future-proofs your most cherished music. You can always create other types of files from a FLAC, but you can never create a true FLAC from a lesser format.
- **General Tier (MP3):** This tier is for the rest of your music library. These files are for general listening, portable devices, and exploring new music. We will use a high-quality lossy format (MP3) that is perceptually transparent—meaning, for most people on most equipment, it's indistinguishable from the original source. This provides an excellent listening experience while using a fraction of the storage space of FLAC.
### 1.2 The Quality Standards
These are the technical rules of the road. Adhering to them ensures that every file entering your "raw materials" folder is of a known, high quality.

|   |   |   |
|---|---|---|
|**Attribute**|**FLAC Standard (Archival Tier)**|**MP3 Standard (General Tier)**|
|**Source**|**Lossless Only** (CD, Hi-Res Web, Vinyl)|**Lossless Only** (CD, Hi-Res Web, Vinyl)|
|**Format**|Free Lossless Audio Codec|MPEG Layer 3|
|**Bit Depth**|16-bit (CD) or 24-bit (Hi-Res)|N/A|
|**Sample Rate**|44.1 kHz (CD) or higher (Hi-Res)|N/A|
|**Encoding**|N/A|**Recommended:** LAME VBR V0 <br> **Alternative:** LAME CBR 320kbps|
|**Verification**|Prioritise rips with `.log`/`.cue` files|Source integrity is key|
**Understanding the Jargon:**
- **Source is Everything:** You cannot create quality out of thin air. A high-quality MP3 or FLAC _must_ be created from a high-quality source. A file converted from a YouTube video into a FLAC is a fake and must be avoided.
- **Bit Depth & Sample Rate:** Think of these like the resolution of a photograph. For audio, the standard CD quality is **16-bit / 44.1 kHz**. This is the target for most of your FLAC files. Higher "Hi-Res" values (like 24-bit / 96 kHz) are only worth seeking if the album was officially sold in that format.
- **Why LAME VBR V0?** LAME is the best MP3 encoder in the world. Its "Variable Bitrate V0" setting is the most intelligent way to create an MP3. It allocates more data to complex parts of a song and less to simple parts, resulting in a file that is slightly smaller than the maximum constant bitrate (320kbps) but with arguably better quality. It is the gold standard for efficient, high-quality MP3s.
### 1.3 The Workflow Overview
This guide focuses solely on the first stage of a two-stage process. It is crucial to understand the separation of duties.
1. **Sourcing (`slskd`):** Think of this as your shopping trip. Your only job is to go out and find the best raw ingredients (audio files) that meet the standards we've defined above. You don't worry about what the files are named or if they have perfect tags. You just focus on audio quality.
2. **Organising (`beets`):** This is the master chef in your kitchen. Once the raw ingredients are downloaded, `beets` takes over. It automatically identifies the music, corrects all the metadata tags, fetches and embeds high-quality album art, renames the files, and places them in your final, perfectly structured library.
By keeping these roles separate, you can focus on one task at a time and leverage the best tool for each job.
## Part 2: Essential Setup for Peak Performance
Think of the Soulseek network as a massive, bustling marketplace. To be a successful participant, you can't just show up; you need to set up your "stall" correctly. This section covers the one-time technical setup that ensures your `slskd` client can communicate freely and effectively with the rest of the network. Getting this right is the foundation for everything else.
### 2.1 Port Forwarding: Opening Your Digital Doors
**The Concept:** Out of the box, most home internet routers act like a one-way street for security. They let you make connections _out_ to the internet, but they block unexpected connections from coming _in_. Soulseek, being a peer-to-peer network, needs a two-way street. You need to be able to connect to others, and they need to be able to connect to you. Port forwarding is the process of telling your router to open a specific digital door and guide any traffic that arrives at that door directly to your `slskd` server.
**Why It's Crucial:**
- **Access More Files:** Many Soulseek users are also behind routers. If neither you nor another user has open ports, you cannot connect to each other at all. By opening your ports, you make yourself reachable, instantly increasing the number of users (and files) you can access.
- **Faster Downloads:** Direct connections are often faster and more stable than connections that have to be relayed or bounced around.
- **Better Queue Position:** Some users' clients are configured to prioritise connections from users with open ports.
**How to Do It (The General Steps):**
1. **Find Your Server's IP Address:** Identify the local IP address of the machine running Docker (e.g., `192.168.1.100`).
2. **Find Your `slskd` Ports:** Look in your `docker-compose.yml` file. You will have a `ports` section that maps a port on your server to a port in the container. The key port is the one for P2P traffic, which is often `5130` or similar. Let's assume it's `5130`.
3. **Log In to Your Router:** Access your home router's administration page via a web browser (commonly `191.168.0.1` or `191.168.1.1`).
4. **Locate Port Forwarding Settings:** This is the trickiest part as every router is different. Look for sections named "Port Forwarding," "NAT," "Virtual Servers," or sometimes "Gaming & Applications."
5. **Create the Rules:** You will need to create a new rule (or two). The key information to enter is:
    - **Application Name:** Anything you like (e.g., "slskd-tcp").
    - **External Port:** `5130`
    - **Internal Port:** `5130`
    - **Protocol:** You should create one rule for **TCP** and another for **UDP**. If your router allows "Both," you can use that.
    - **Device/IP Address:** The local IP address of your server you found in step 1.
6. **Verify Your Work:** After saving the rules, restart `slskd`. In the Web UI, go to the "Soulseek" or "Diagnostics" tab. There should be a network status indicator. Your goal is for it to say **"Open"**.
### 2.2 Upload Configuration: The Network's Currency
**The Concept:** The Soulseek network operates on a social economy of sharing. The files you share, and your ability to upload them to others, are your "currency." A user who shares nothing (a "leecher") will find their access and download speeds are poor. By being a generous uploader, you become a valuable member of the community, and the system often rewards you for it.
**Why It's Crucial:**
- **Queue Priority:** When you want to download a file from someone, you are placed in their queue. Many users' clients will automatically move you up the queue if they can see you have files they might want, or if you have free upload slots for them to use.
- **Access to Locked Shares:** Some users lock their files, only granting access to users on their list or those who meet certain sharing criteria. Being a good sharer increases your chances of being granted access.
**How to Do It (Actionable Settings):**
- **Set Generous Upload Slots:** An upload "slot" is a single simultaneous connection you offer to another user. For a server that is on 24/7, you can be quite generous.
    - **Where:** This setting is in your `slskd.yml` configuration file or in the Web UI settings under "Uploads."
    - **What:** A good starting point is **10** slots. If you have a robust internet connection, you can comfortably increase this to 20 or more.
- **Set Sufficient Upload Bandwidth:** Do not starve your client of upload speed.
    - **Where:** This is also in your config file or UI settings.
    - **What:** First, run an internet speed test to find your maximum upload speed. Then, allocate a significant portion of that to `slskd`. A good rule of thumb is to allocate **70-80%** of your total upload bandwidth. This ensures your uploads are fast for others, but leaves you enough bandwidth for your own browsing or streaming without slowdowns.
## Part 3: The Sourcing Process - From Search to Download
This is the active, hands-on part of the workflow. The mindset to adopt is that you are not just querying a database; you are exploring the personal, hand-curated music collections of thousands of individuals. This section details how to do that effectively.
### 3.1 The Tiered Search Methodology
This structured approach takes you from simple lookups to advanced discovery.
- **Tier 1: Standard Precise Search:** This is your foundation.
    - **Action:** Enclose the artist and album in quotes (e.g., `"The Beatles" "Abbey Road"`).
    - **Why:** Without quotes, you are searching for files containing "The" AND "Beatles" AND "Abbey" AND "Road," which can return messy, unrelated results. Quotes search for the _exact phrase_, giving you much cleaner initial results.
- **Tier 2: Format-Specific Search:** This is your first layer of quality control.
    - **Action:** Add your target format to the query (e.g., `"Abbey Road" flac`).
    - **Why:** You are telling the network, "Don't even bother showing me results unless the filename or path explicitly mentions my desired format." This dramatically reduces the number of low-quality results you have to sift through.
- **Tier 3: Structural Search:** This is where you evolve from searching for files to searching for _collectors_.
    - **Action:** Search for common folder names (e.g., search for the directory `"The Beatles [FLAC]"`) or for verification files (`"Abbey Road" .log`).
    - **Why:** You are looking for clues that a user is meticulous. A user who names their folders neatly or keeps the ripping logs is highly likely to have high-quality, complete albums. Finding one such folder can yield an entire discography in one click, which is far more efficient than searching for a dozen albums individually.
- **Tier 4: Obscurity Search:** This is your strategy when you hit a brick wall looking for a rare item.
    - **Action:** Search for only the artist name (`"The Beta Band"`). Find a user who has some of their tracks, then use the **"Browse User's Files"** feature.
    - **Why:** The rare track you want may be poorly tagged or have a strange filename. The only way to find it is to manually explore the collection of a fan who is likely to have it. The "Browse User's Files" function is the golden key to discovery on Soulseek.

### 3.2 Automated Quality Control: Using `slskd` Filters

The search brings a list of potential files to your door; the filter acts as a doorman, only letting in the ones that meet your strict standards.

**How to Use Filters:** In the `slskd` Web UI, after you type your search query, you will see a "Filter" text box to the right. This is where you paste the rules.

- **Filter for Sourcing FLAC Files:**
    
    ```
    islossless minbitrate:700 minbitdepth:16 minfilesinfolder:7 log cue -transcode
    ```
    
    **What this filter does, piece by piece:**
    
    - `islossless`: Only shows results that are identified as lossless (FLAC, WAV, ALAC, etc.).
        
    - `minbitrate:700`: Your weapon against fakes. It discards any "FLAC" file with a suspiciously low bitrate, which is the hallmark of a transcoded MP3.
        
    - `minbitdepth:16`: Sets the quality floor at CD-level (16-bit), ensuring you don't get lower-quality sources. It will also allow 24-bit files.
        
    - `minfilesinfolder:7`: A simple check for album completeness, filtering out single tracks in messy folders.
        
    - `log cue`: Prioritises results that include these words, strongly indicating a perfect CD rip.
        
    - `-transcode`: Excludes anything explicitly named as a transcode.
        
- **Filter for Sourcing MP3 Files:**
    
    ```
    islossy minbitrate:220 minfilesinfolder:7 -128k -192k -youtube -soundcloud
    ```
    
    **What this filter does, piece by piece:**
    
    - `islossy`: Only shows results identified as lossy (MP3, AAC, etc.).
        
    - `minbitrate:220`: The core of your MP3 quality control. This cleanly eliminates all 128kbps and 192kbps files, while being low enough to catch all genuine V0 and 320kbps files.
        
    - `minfilesinfolder:7`: Same check for album completeness.
        
    - `-128k -192k`: A backup rule to exclude files that have low bitrates written in their names.
        
    - `-youtube -soundcloud`: Excludes files that are very likely from poor-quality streaming sources.
        

> **Pro-Tip:** Remember that filters are rules, and sometimes rules need to be bent. If you are searching for a 3-track EP, you might need to temporarily change `minfilesinfolder:7` to `minfilesinfolder:3` for that one search.

## Part 4: The Long Game - Advanced Strategies

Once you have mastered the basics, you can evolve your workflow to be more efficient and proactive. These strategies will save you time and help you discover music you didn't even know you were looking for.

### 4.1 Curate Your User List

**The Concept:** Your user list (or "buddy list") is the single most powerful feature for long-term success. Instead of treating every search as a cold start across the entire network, you can build a trusted, private network of high-quality sources whose musical tastes align with yours. This transforms Soulseek from a simple search engine into a curated discovery platform.

**The Workflow:**

1. **Find a Good Source:** When a search leads you to a file that perfectly matches your quality standards, don't just download and leave. This is your opportunity.
    
2. **Investigate:** Right-click the user and select **"Browse User's Files."** Look at their collection. Is it well-organised? Do they have other albums you're interested in? Is the quality consistent?
    
3. **Add to List:** If the user's library looks good, add them to your user list. Most clients let you add a note; use it to remind yourself why you added them (e.g., "Great collection of 90s indie FLACs").
    
4. **Proactive Browsing:** Now, when you're looking for new music, your first step should be to browse the shares of your top 5-10 users _before_ you run a public search. This is often faster and yields more reliable results.
    

### 4.2 Leverage the Community

**The Concept:** Soulseek is more than just a file-sharing network; it's a collection of communities. The chat rooms are hubs for enthusiasts of specific genres, from ambient techno to Norwegian black metal. The users in these rooms are often passionate collectors and experts.

**How to Use It:**

- **Find a Room:** Explore the list of available chat rooms in your client. Find one that matches a genre you love.
    
- **Lurk and Learn:** You don't have to chat. Simply join the room and look at the user list.
    
- **Discover:** Pick a user from the room list and browse their files. You will often find incredibly deep and well-curated collections that are laser-focused on that genre, containing rare B-sides, bootlegs, and albums you've never heard of. It's a fantastic tool for musical discovery.
    

### 4.3 The Ultimate Optimisation: Automation with Lidarr

**The Concept:** For the ultimate hands-off approach, you can automate the entire sourcing process. Lidarr is a companion application (part of the popular "arr" suite) that acts as the brain for your music collection.

**How it Transforms Your Workflow:**

1. **You Tell Lidarr What You Want:** You add an artist to Lidarr and tell it which albums you're missing. You also configure a quality profile (e.g., "I only want V0 MP3 or FLAC").
    
2. **Lidarr Commands `slskd`:** Lidarr connects to `slskd`'s API. It periodically tells `slskd` to search for the albums on your wanted list that match your quality profile.
    
3. **The Pipeline:** When `slskd` finds and finishes a download, Lidarr is notified. It then automatically sends the downloaded files to `beets` (or handles the import itself).
    
4. **The Result:** Your library grows automatically with high-quality files without you needing to manually search for anything. You simply maintain your "wanted" list in Lidarr, and the system does the rest. This is the pinnacle of an efficient, automated sourcing workflow.

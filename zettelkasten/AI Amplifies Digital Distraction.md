Andrew Sullivan's 2016 essay described a "distraction sickness" stemming from the overwhelming digital landscape, characterized by a feeling of being immersed in a "cacophonous crowd" of information even when alone, ultimately leading to a "way of not-living." In the current era, the proliferation of generative AI profoundly amplifies this phenomenon. The constant influx of AI-generated content, notifications, and the pressure to integrate AI into all aspects of life further intensifies digital saturation, jeopardizing our capacity for deep work, critical thinking, and genuine human connection. This challenge extends beyond simple screen time management, becoming a fundamental issue for maintaining cognitive and emotional well-being in an AI-pervasive environment. 

  

Literature Note: "MY DISTRACTION SICKNESS - and YOURS" (Sullivan, 2016)

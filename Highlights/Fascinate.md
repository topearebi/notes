Step 2: Thematic Clusters
Theme 1: The Biological Imperative of Irrationality
 * Fisher says that our brains are literally “built to fall in love” because it’s in our evolutionary best interest not to think clearly... or else we might come to our senses and avoid the inconvenience of child rearing altogether.
 * Lust offers four pillars: Stop thinking, start feeling. Make the ordinary more emotional.
 * If your message must compel people to want something—really, really want it, despite rational evidence to the contrary—employ the factors of sight, sound, taste, touch, and scent.
Theme 2: The Architecture of Subconscious Learning
 * Though the patient had no recollection of his time in the maze... Each time the patient made his way through the maze, he did it differently. Specifically, he did it faster. Without actively remembering the maze, he was subconsciously learning.
 *  * Memory, the scientists learned, works whether we realize it or not. All day, we passively take in messages... Enough messages, drilled in over decades, will eventually shape our actions.
Theme 3: The Failure of "Awareness" and Rationality
 * Interest is not enough. Neither is awareness, intent to purchase, or having share-of-mind.
 * We’re living hyperlinks... networking, but not connecting, doing things so that we can tell others we just did them.
 * The MP3 player wasn’t quite so fascinating until the iPod triggered lust.
 * Apple’s sleep light resembles the human characteristic of a beating heart.
Theme 4: Social Currency and Advocacy
 * People don’t want to connect with brands. They want to connect with each other. Fascinating companies create more opportunities for people to connect with each other, through the brand.
 * It’s not about marketing a message—it’s about getting the market to create messages about you.
Step 3: The Idea Incubator
 * Title: The Evolutionary "Inconvenience": Why Great Brands Must Suspend Logic
   * The Hook: Just as evolution "tricks" us into the hardships of parenting through chemical love, successful brands must bypass the prefrontal cortex to make "inconvenient" or expensive choices feel like biological necessities.
   * The Evidence: Fisher's quote on the brain being "built to fall in love" to avoid "coming to our senses."
 * Title: The Amnesiac’s Maze: Designing for the Subconscious Consumer
   * The Hook: If consumers "passively take in messages" without conscious recall, brand strategy should focus on "pathway building" (consistency and frequency) rather than one-off "memorable" stunts.
   * The Evidence: The case study of the chronic amnesiac learning the maze through repetition without conscious memory.
 * Title: From Hyperlinks to Heartbeats: The Anthropomorphism of Objects
   * The Hook: Why hardware that mimics human vitals (like the pulsing sleep light) wins over superior technical specs in a "networked but disconnected" society.
   * The Evidence: Apple's beating heart sleep light and the iPod triggering "lust."
Step 4: The Contextual Bridge
 * Neuroscience: The amnesiac in the maze illustrates the difference between Explicit Memory (facts/events) and Implicit Memory (skills/habits). In a world of "distracted consumers," should marketers stop trying to be remembered (explicit) and start trying to be "learned" as a habit (implicit)?
 * Sociology: The text claims we are "living hyperlinks" who network without connecting. How does the "Lust" trigger—which demands sensory engagement (sight, sound, taste)—act as an antidote to the "digital thinning" of human experience?
 * Ethics: If the goal of fascination is to make someone want something "despite rational evidence to the contrary," where is the line between persuasion (helping a consumer find a solution) and manipulation (exploiting evolutionary "glitches" like the two-year romantic fog)?

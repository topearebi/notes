My earliest memory is picture of me, looking at a picture of me, wondering how I broke my arm at such a young age. I almost remember it when my mum narrates the story to me. Me climbing high up on that table. Unafraid. Unbothered even as my father paid me no mind as my mother calls out from the kitchen. Till I am on the floor.

My second memory, I am falling, and falling, and falling. I am high up again on the shoulders of a classmate. We are outside. We are running. We are winning. I am in the hospital learning that POP means plaster of paris. That it looks a bit like a huge block of chalk. I didn't remember that from the first time.

I have another memory of falling. Over and over again. I am running, scared, thrilled. Running has always made me happy. Yet as my knee crashes into the sharp edge of the stair. As my right foot slips out from under me. As I take that leap away from the dog who in the next 5 seconds will turn around, uninterested, like she finds me unworthy for having such an accident. Some part of me knows I have just run the fastest I ever will from that day.

I have so many regrets. Today I dream about them all. So many times when I was afraid to fall. Afraid that people would turn away, like the dog. Afraid that I was no longer fat enough. Strong enough. Smart enough. That I have already broken it all.
So I dream that my first love would have loved me, even though I never jumped. I dream that my father pays me attention, he says sorry. He says well done. He lifts me up on his shoulder. And we never fall.
